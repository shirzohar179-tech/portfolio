Case study 03 · Responsive web · Real client

A glass engraving studio you can book without a phone call

A small studio in Israel runs glass engraving workshops, and everything (prices, dates, group sizes) lived in phone calls and Instagram messages. I designed a Hebrew, mobile-first website where people can see every detail up front and book a date on their own.

01 — The briefA beautiful workshop, hidden behind a phone call

The studio's owner asked for a website that feels as aesthetic as the work itself, and that lets people schedule a workshop easily. The site had to answer, on its own, every question people usually ask before booking:

  • How much does it cost, and what's the minimum number of participants?
  • How long does it take, and what's included?
  • Where is it, and who is the instructor?
  • What will I actually make? (photos of real pieces)
  • What did other people think? (recommendations)
  • How do I reach you? (contact details, Instagram, and a form to leave details)

My working hypothesis: people don't drop off because they aren't interested, but because the key facts are hidden. When price, duration and group size need a phone call, "maybe later" becomes "never".

The psychology behind it

Hidden prices feel like risk

Ambiguity aversion (Ellsberg, 1961): people avoid options whose outcome they can't estimate, even when the option may be good. A price you have to ask for feels riskier than a price you can see, so the site shows every fact on the card, before anyone has to ask.

The IKEA effect (Norton, Mochon & Ariely, 2012) explains the product itself: people value what they made with their own hands. So the site sells the experience of making, not just finished glasses, and the booking flow lets people say what they'd like to engrave.

03 — Design principlesFour rules for every screen

  1. Every fact up front. Price, duration, minimum participants and what's included sit on each workshop card, never behind a click or a call.
  2. Book in four steps, no account. Workshop → date & time → vessels → details. Only available days can be tapped, and the running total is always visible.
  3. The price follows the vessel. The studio prices by the glass item each person engraves, so every participant picks one vessel from a visible price list, and the number of vessels is the number of participants. One choice replaces two separate questions.
  4. Groups are a conversation, not a checkout. Bachelorette parties and team days need coordination, so they get a short request form instead of instant booking.

04 — StructureFrom wireframes to one clear path

I started with grayscale mobile wireframes to settle the order of the home page: what it is → next available dates → workshops → the instructor → gallery → recommendations → groups → location → contact. Putting the next open dates second means someone who already knows they want to come can book straight from the home page.

05 — Final designGlass, light and a fine line

The visual language borrows from the craft: a warm paper background, a deep sea-glass green for every action, a small touch of brass for details like "last spot left". Type is one modern family, IBM Plex Sans Hebrew: a light weight for large headings, echoing a fine engraved line, and regular and semibold for text. Everything is right-to-left, touch targets are at least 44px and text meets WCAG AA contrast.

Because the price depends on what you engrave, the home page shows the vessels (wine glass, beer glass, bottle, jar, mirror) with their prices and a worked example, so nobody reaches the booking flow surprised by the total.

The site is live as a working demo: the home page, the booking flow (with a real calendar and per-vessel pricing) and the groups page. It's built in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, right-to-left, and adapts from phone to desktop.

For groups, a separate page lists the occasions (bachelorette parties, birthdays, team building), shows the facts for groups, and ends in a three-question request form, with WhatsApp and Instagram as alternatives for people who prefer to talk.
